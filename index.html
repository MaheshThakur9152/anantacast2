One burnout, hour by hour

The kiln’s day

Lost-wax casting runs on heat and patience. Here’s how a flask travels through the kiln, from cold plaster to molten metal. Typical figures; every flask, metal and kiln is a little different.

  1. Hour 00

    Invest

    Your wax tree stands in a steel flask. We pour investment plaster around it, pull the air out under vacuum, and let it set hard.

  2. Hour 02

    Dewax

    The flask goes into the kiln upside down. At around 150 °C the wax softens and runs out, leaving a perfect hollow of your piece.

  3. Hour 04

    Climb

    A slow climb and a hold. Water leaves the plaster gently so the mould doesn’t crack.

  4. Hour 06

    Burnout

    Full heat. Every last trace of wax and resin burns away and the mould cures hard enough to take metal.

  5. Hour 09

    Settle

    The kiln comes down to casting temperature, matched to the metal and how fine the piece is.

  6. Hour 09

    Pour

    The metal melts in the crucible and goes in under vacuum, filling every claw, gallery and letter of the hollow.

  7. Hour 10

    Break out

    The flask rests, then hits the water. The plaster bursts away and the tree comes out in metal, ready to be cut and cleaned.

The metals

Four colours of fire

Every karat is pure metal plus an alloy. What goes into that alloy decides the colour, the hardness and how it pours.

Yellow gold

Gold + silver + copper

The classic. Balanced silver and copper keep the warm colour true. Lovely for bands, kadas and traditional work.

Rose gold

Gold + more copper

Extra copper blushes the gold pink and makes it a little harder. A favourite for modern rings and fine chains.

White gold

Gold + palladium or nickel

White metals bleach the gold pale. Usually finished with rhodium plating for a bright white shine around stones.

Sterling silver

92.5% silver + 7.5% copper

Bright, affordable and quick to work. Perfect for samples, collections and everyday pieces.
